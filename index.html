What is going on

The network is a function estimating . Each hidden unit computes an affine function of the previous layer followed by a fixed nonlinearity, so a first-layer unit is a soft step across a line in the plane. Later layers form nonlinear combinations of those features, and the tiles in the network diagram show exactly what each unit has learned to respond to. Training is gradient descent on the cross-entropy, which is the negative log-likelihood of a Bernoulli model, so the heatmap is a calibrated probability only to the extent that the model and data allow it.

Why depth helps on the spiral: a one-hidden-layer net with tanh units is a weighted sum of ridges, and the boundary it can draw with a handful of units is a few gently curved pieces. Composition is more economical: each layer folds and stretches the representation produced by the one before, and the number of linear regions of a ReLU net grows exponentially with depth but only polynomially with width. Two interleaved arms that wind almost twice around the origin need many such folds, which a stack of three or four small layers provides with far fewer parameters than a single wide layer.

The learning rate sets the step size relative to the curvature of the loss. Near a minimum with Hessian eigenvalue , gradient descent contracts only if . Above that it overshoots and the loss oscillates or diverges. Too small and progress is slow, especially along flat directions. Momentum averages past gradients, which speeds travel along consistent directions and multiplies the effective step by roughly . Adam divides each coordinate by a running RMS of its gradient, making steps roughly scale-invariant per parameter. That is why it copes well with badly scaled layers and flat starts. ReLU units have an extra failure mode: a large step can push a unit's pre-activation below zero for every input, after which its gradient is identically zero and it never recovers.

L2 regularization adds to the objective, which shows up as weight decay in SGD. Small weights keep tanh units in their near-linear range and bound the network's Lipschitz constant, so the boundary cannot bend sharply around individual noisy points. With few, noisy training samples and a large net, the unregularized model drives train loss toward zero by carving islands around outliers while test loss rises: that is overfitting. Penalizing weights trades a little training fit for a smoother, better-generalizing boundary. Note that L2 is implemented as part of the gradient, so with Adam it is the coupled L2 variant, not AdamW's decoupled decay.
